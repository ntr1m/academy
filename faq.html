FAQ

Before You
Start.

Everything you need to know before enquiring about lessons.

Do I need skating experience?

No. Complete beginners are welcome. Every new student begins with a Trial Session so the coach can understand their starting point.

Who can join?

NTR1M coaches children from age 3+ as well as teens and adults. Programmes range from beginner through advanced and competitive development.

Where are lessons held?

Lessons are conducted islandwide at suitable skating locations. NTR1M comes to you rather than operating from one fixed academy venue.

How much do lessons cost?

Private coaching starts from S$50/hour, group coaching from S$40/hour/person and competitive development from S$80/hour. Rates vary depending on location, duration and arrangement.

Do I need to buy skates?

You need suitable inline skates and a helmet for lessons. Equipment rental is available from S$15/session, subject to availability. Protective gear is recommended.

Do I have to compete?

No. Competition is optional. Students can skate recreationally and progress through the curriculum without entering competitions.

What happens in a first lesson?

The one-hour Trial Session combines coaching and assessment. The coach looks at ability, confidence, coachability, attitude and interest while introducing suitable skills.

What happens if it rains?

We first attempt to continue under suitable shelter. If that is not possible, the affected lesson is refunded. If more than half the lesson has already been completed, it is charged as completed.

How quickly will I progress?

There is no fixed timeline. Progress depends on factors such as starting ability, lesson frequency, practice and individual learning pace.

Private or group coaching?

Private coaching offers the most individual attention and is recommended for many beginners. Group coaching is suitable when skaters of similar ability can train effectively together.

How does progression work?

NTR1M uses six curriculum levels from Foundation to Master. Progression is based on coach discretion and demonstrated competency rather than formal pass/fail tests.
